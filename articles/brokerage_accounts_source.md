# Types of Brokerage Accounts

Source: [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/how-stock-markets-work/types-brokerage-accounts)
Reviewed November 5, 2026.
Capture: selected educational summary; not the full source article.

In a cash brokerage account, the investor pays the full amount for securities purchased. In a margin account, the brokerage can lend money to buy securities, using the portfolio as collateral. Borrowing creates interest costs and additional risk.

If the value of collateral declines, a broker may require more funds or sell securities to cover a shortfall. The broker can sell without consulting the investor and can act before a stated margin-call deadline. It can also change its maintenance requirements. Margin borrowing is therefore different from simply investing cash already available in the account.
