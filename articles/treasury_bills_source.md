# Treasury Bills

Source: [U.S. Department of the Treasury](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/treasury-bills/)
Reviewed November 5, 2026.
Capture: selected educational summary; not the full source article.

Treasury bills are U.S. Treasury marketable securities with terms ranging from four to 52 weeks. They are sold at a discount or at face value. At maturity, Treasury pays the face value. The difference between the purchase price and the amount received at maturity represents the interest for a discounted bill.

A holder can retain a bill until maturity or sell it before maturity. Bills differ from longer-term Treasury notes and bonds in their short maturities and payment structure. Treasury also issues cash management bills for variable terms through banks, brokers, or dealers.
