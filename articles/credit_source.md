# Credit Reports and Scores

**Source:** [Washington State Department of Financial Institutions (DFI)](https://dfi.wa.gov/financial-education/information/credit-reports)  
**Reviewed:** October 30, 2026  
**Audience:** Consumers learning how credit reports and scores work

## Credit reports and credit scores

A credit report records information about a person's use of credit. It can include credit accounts, balances, credit limits, payment history, negative marks, and recent inquiries. A credit score is a number calculated from information in a credit report. Lenders and other organizations may use reports or scores when making decisions.

## Why credit reports and scores matter

DFI explains that credit reports and scores can affect whether someone gets a loan, rents an apartment, or sometimes gets a job. Lenders, landlords, insurers, and some employers may review a credit report when making decisions. These are possible uses; a credit score alone does not determine every decision.

## Checking a credit report

Consumers can obtain free reports from the three major credit reporting companies through AnnualCreditReport.com, the federally authorized source identified by DFI. Checking your own report does not affect your credit score. Reviewing a report can help you notice information that is inaccurate or unfamiliar.

## Factors related to credit scores

Scoring models may consider payment history, amounts owed and credit utilization, length of credit history, credit mix, and applications for new credit. Different scoring models can produce different scores, so a person may see more than one score.

## Improving and correcting credit information

The DFI page describes paying bills on time, reducing balances, avoiding several new accounts at once, and checking reports for errors as ways to build or improve a score. If a report contains inaccurate information, consumers can dispute it with the credit reporting company and the company that supplied the information.
