# High-Yield Bonds

Source: [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/bonds-or-fixed-income-products/high)
Reviewed November 5, 2026.
Capture: selected educational summary; not the full source article.

High-yield bonds pay higher interest rates to compensate investors for a greater risk of default than investment-grade bonds. They are also called non-investment-grade or speculative bonds. A higher promised yield is compensation for risk, rather than assurance of a better realized return.

Issuers may have substantial debt, financial difficulties, or a short and unproven operating record. Such conditions can prevent them from obtaining investment-grade ratings. If the issuer cannot make payments, investors may lose interest or principal. Comparing yields therefore also requires attention to the issuer's ability to repay.
