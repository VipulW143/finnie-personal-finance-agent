# Money Market Funds

Source: [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-5)
Reviewed November 5, 2026.
Capture: selected educational summary; not the full source article.

Money market funds are mutual funds that invest in liquid, short-term debt, cash, and cash equivalents. They have historically offered lower returns and lower risk than many other mutual funds. Their dividends generally reflect short-term interest rates.

Retail and government money market funds generally seek a stable net asset value, commonly one dollar per share. Institutional prime and institutional tax-exempt funds use a floating net asset value. Shares are redeemed with the fund rather than traded like shares of an ETF. A stable-value objective describes the fund's design; it should not be read as a promise of investment returns.
