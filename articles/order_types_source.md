# Types of Orders

Source: [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/how-stock-markets-work/types-orders)
Reviewed November 5, 2026.
Capture: selected educational summary; not the full source article.

A market order seeks to buy or sell a security immediately. It generally prioritizes execution, but the execution price is not guaranteed. The price shown on a screen may differ from the price received.

A limit order specifies a price or better: a buy limit can execute only at the limit or lower, and a sell limit only at the limit or higher. A stop order becomes a market order when the stop price is reached. Its trigger price is therefore different from a guaranteed execution price. These order types express different choices about price conditions and execution.
