# Corporate Bonds

Source: [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/bonds-or-fixed-income-products)
Reviewed November 5, 2026.
Capture: selected educational summary; not the full source article.

Corporations issue bonds to borrow money. Buying a corporate bond does not confer ownership of the company. Instead, the investor has a contractual claim to interest and principal according to the bond's terms. Corporate bondholders generally have priority over shareholders in bankruptcy, but this does not ensure that all money will be recovered.

Credit quality reflects an issuer's ability to meet its obligations. Ratings distinguish investment-grade debt from lower-rated debt, but a rating does not eliminate risk. A fixed-rate bond's coupon does not change with market interest rates, although its market price can change.
