# How traditional pretax 401(k) contributions affect taxable wages

Source: IRS Topic 424, 401(k) plans
https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc424
Reviewed November 4, 2026.

Eligible employees can generally elect to contribute a portion of compensation to a 401(k) on a pretax basis. These elective deferrals generally are excluded from federal taxable wages in Form W-2 box 1, and are not reported as wages on Form 1040. This explains how traditional pretax contributions reduce current federal taxable income.

The deferrals are still included in wages subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes. Pretax treatment for federal income tax does not remove these payroll taxes. Elective deferrals are also reported as an information item in Form W-2 box 12.

Designated Roth contributions are made on an after-tax basis. Contribution limits and plan rules apply.
