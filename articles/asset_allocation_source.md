# Asset Allocation

Source: [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation)
Reviewed November 5, 2026.
Capture: selected educational summary; not the full source article.

Asset allocation means dividing investments among asset classes, such as stocks, bonds, and cash. The choice depends on the investor's time horizon and ability and willingness to accept losses. A shorter time horizon can affect the amount of risk that can be taken.

Market movements can change the portfolio's allocation even without new purchases. For example, a stock allocation that started at 60 percent can grow to a larger share after stocks outperform. Rebalancing brings the portfolio back toward its intended mix. Asset allocation manages the mix of risks; it does not guarantee profits or prevent all losses.
