# What is compound interest?

Source: [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/information/youth/teachers-classroom-resources/what-compound-interest)
Reviewed November 4, 2026.

Compound interest means earning interest on previously earned interest as well as on the original principal.

In the SEC's illustration, $100 earns 5% annually and the interest remains in the account. After one year, the balance is $105. In the second year it earns $5 on the original $100 and $0.25 on the first year's $5 interest, giving $110.25. This is a hypothetical fixed-interest illustration.

The Rule of 72 gives an approximate doubling time: divide 72 by the expected annual percentage return. At 9%, the estimate is eight years. This is an approximation based on the assumed rate.
